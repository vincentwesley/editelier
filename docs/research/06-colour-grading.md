# 06 — Colour grading: "really good colour-graded edits" in one tap

Research date: 2026-11-26. Scope: Editelier's end goal as clarified by the owner — easily make vibrant, cinematic colour on video **and** photos (reference: the YouTube creator "eyes of leo" and similar), via one-tap looks with depth for tweaking. Builds on D5 (linear-light Rec.709 f16 compositor), R05 §A3 (colour management, BT.2390 HDR tonemap) and R03 (looks-first UX). Every external claim links its source; lines marked **Rec:** are our engineering recommendations. Lines marked **Measured:** come from our own analysis of the owner's reference frames.

**Clean-room note.** The owner's four reference frames are copyrighted YouTube screenshots. They were analysed numerically only; nothing from them (pixels, crops, thumbnails) goes into the repo, tests or docs. Commercial looks, LUTs, film profiles and presets named below are described for UX lessons only; Editelier authors its own looks from parameters and ships no third-party LUTs.

**Measured, frame by frame:**
- **F1 "sunny film / digicam".** Overexposed by roughly a stop with hard clipping (bimodal histogram: 18 % of pixels in the darkest eighth, 18.5 % in the brightest). True blacks but with blue lifted most (floor B 13 vs R 3). A strong cross-process split: blue-violet shadows, cyan mids, **yellow-green highlights**. Foliage and highlights fall in the yellow-green band (32 % of pixels). One hero colour, red, is pushed to by far the highest chroma of any frame while it covers only 2 % of the image. Around clipped areas the image turns warm (the red-minus-blue difference jumps ~8×), consistent with added halation/bloom. Grain is visible by eye but not measurable through compression.
- **F2 "muted teal street".** Low-key (median 49), lifted black floor at 13 (a gentle fade), a soft shoulder (nothing clips; top ~221). Very low overall chroma (7.2, less than half of F1). Cool, slightly teal blacks (R floor below G/B), blue-cyan mids. The only saturated things are the warm autumn accents, at about 2× the median chroma: selective saturation.
- **F3 "teal night interior".** 81 % of pixels are shadows; the most lifted, matte floor of the set (16). The window highlights are held (no clipping) and pushed cyan-teal (C* 17). Shadows are nearly neutral with a teal bias (R floor 6 vs G/B 15). The red hoodie barely registers: the grade lets the dark exposure kill it rather than pushing it.
- **F4 "warm skin close-up".** Very low key and soft: the brightest 0.5 % only reaches 150/255, so the grade (or exposure) leaves no whites; 5th–95th percentile spans just 14–106. Warm throughout (orange shadows, amber mids) with a slight green-teal bias in the deepest blacks. **Skin** (face region, hue-filtered): Lab hue ≈ 78°, chroma 11, L* 32; on a Rec.709 vectorscope the median sits at ≈ 144°, about 20° from the classic 123° skin-tone line toward yellow ([Boris FX/VEGAS docs](https://cdn.borisfx.com/borisfx/Documentation/vegas/2026/en/content/topics/8-design/video_scope_settings.htm)). So: golden/olive skin, low saturation, not orange. The "teal fill" is local (background window, eye catchlights: teal is only 2.4 % of pixels), not a global cast.

**What the four share:** a lifted, slightly cool/teal black floor (R lowest), restrained overall saturation with one or two hero colours (red clothing, warm leaves), warm-vs-cool separation between subject and environment, and highlights that either roll off softly (F2–F4) or blow out with a warm halo (F1). That is the recipe in §1 in miniature.

## 1. What "cinematic / vibrant" actually means

**Plain words:** a cinematic grade decides what the viewer looks at. It makes the darks and brights feel deliberate (no harsh digital clipping), separates the subject from the background with warm-vs-cool colour, and lets only one or two colours really sing while everything else calms down. "Vibrant" in creator speak rarely means "everything saturated"; it means a few colours pop against a controlled background.

**Technically:**
- **Contrast S-curve with highlight roll-off.** Contrast around a mid-grey pivot, a toe that shapes the blacks and a shoulder that compresses highlights gradually instead of clipping. darktable's scene-referred tone mappers (filmic rgb, sigmoid, AgX) all exist to "remap the tonal range" this way, and it warns against stacking two tone mappers because that "produce[s] unpredictable tones and colors" ([darktable sigmoid](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/5.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/sigmoid/), [filmic rgb](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/development/en/module-reference/processing-modules/filmic-rgb/)). Sigmoid's "rgb ratio" mode maps the RGB triplet uniformly to preserve hue ([same](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/5.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/sigmoid/)).
- **Black point / fade.** Lifting the output black (F2: 13, F3: 16 of 255) gives the "matte" printed-photo feel; Fujifilm describes its Nostalgic Neg simulation as "amber tinted highlights and rich shadow tone for printed photo look" ([Fujifilm](https://www.fujifilm-x.com/global/film-simulation/)).
- **Colour separation (complementary).** Teal-orange works because skin sits in the orange range, so pushing shadows/backgrounds toward teal makes faces stand out ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2017/02/23/orange-teal-look-popular-hollywood/), [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/features/why-use-orange-teal-grading)). Critics note it was tuned around a narrow range of lighter skin and over-pushing can over-saturate deeper skin ([Morphic glossary](https://morphic.com/ai-glossary/teal-and-orange)). **Rec:** separation, not a fixed teal-orange: our looks rotate background hues away from the skin band, whatever the skin tone.
- **Selective saturation (vibrance vs saturation).** Saturation lifts all colours equally; vibrance lifts muted colours more and protects already-saturated colours and skin tones ([Adobe: make colours pop](https://www.adobe.com/ca/learn/lightroom-cc/web/make-colors-pop), [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/au/tutorials/saturation-vs-vibrance-whats-the-difference-between-these-photo-editing-tools)). Measured: our "vibrant" references have *low* mean chroma (7–17) with one hero hue at 2–3× the median. **Rec:** looks lower global saturation and boost 1–2 hue bands.
- **Skin-tone protection.** Well-balanced skin clusters along the ~123° I-line on a vectorscope regardless of complexion; only distance from centre changes ([OpenRV docs](https://lifeart.github.io/openrv-web/docs/scopes/vectorscope.html), [Kdenlive](https://docs.kdenlive.org/en/tips_and_tricks/scopes/vectorscope_i_and_q_lines.html)). LUTs "applied over the whole frame" change every pixel, including faces; the usual beginner failure is "really orange" faces ([Frame.io LUTs 101](https://blog.frame.io/2019/08/12/luts-101/), [Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/news/you-should-avoid-these-5-color-grading-mistakes-at-all-costs/)).
- **Warm/cool split toning.** Cool shadows, warm highlights (or the reverse) — the core of the teal-orange idea ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2017/02/23/orange-teal-look-popular-hollywood/)). F1 shows the cross-processed variant (blue shadows, yellow-green highlights).
- **Film emulation.** Print-film density: print emulation shows "the density and color behavior of the film"; on ungraded footage it looks darker, contrastier and less saturated, and it reduces "colour contrast and hue variation" ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2014/01/13/print-film-emulation-luts/), [Mixing Light](https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/what-makes-film-print-emulation-luts-special-and-how-do-you-use-them)). **Halation:** light passes the film base, reflects off the plate and re-exposes the red-sensitive layer, so it is a red/orange edge glow around hard bright edges, distinct from neutral diffuse **bloom** ([Dehancer](https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/halation/), [VSCO](https://www.vsco.co/learn/halation-vs-bloom-vs-lens-flare), [CineD](https://www.cined.com/chasing-the-glow-understanding-halation-and-why-filmmakers-keep-coming-back-to-it/)). **Grain** is best modelled from image content, not a scanned overlay; a physically based Boolean grain model with code exists ([Newson et al., IPOL 2017](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2017/192/)) and a much faster follow-up ([ACM 2023](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3592127)). **Gate weave / flicker** shift the frame and pulse exposure; reviewers find them "overly dramatic" on digital ([Frame.io on Film Look Creator](https://blog.frame.io/2024/08/15/what-is-resolves-new-film-look-creator-plugin/), [Tuts+](https://photography.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-use-davinci-resolves-new-film-look-creator--cms-108691)). **Vignette**: subtle edge darkening (offered by Dehancer and others, [Dehancer shop](https://dehancer.com/shop)).
- **Aspect ratio / letterbox.** A 2.39:1 matte reads as "film" instantly; it's a crop, not a grade. Note our frames 2–4 are plain 16:9.
- **Shot matching.** Resolve's Shot Match brings a scene's clips "to a shared starting point", not a finished look; it matches colour, contrast and brightness only ([Larry Jordan](https://larryjordan.com/articles/how-to-color-match-clips-in-davinci-resolve-20/), [Filmora](https://filmora.wondershare.com/video-editing-tips/match-color-in-video-clips.html)). Third-party matchers can beat it ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/cinematch-color-matching/)).

## 2. How the creators do it (camera vs grade)

- **"eyes of leo"** is the YouTube channel @shotbywang ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@shotbywang)). No public interview, gear list or grading breakdown was found in several searches; the channel page exposes no tool list. Everything we know about the look comes from §0. **Kyle Ng, Jonathan Fischer, Hun Kim:** no verifiable public grading workflow found; not cited. **Sam Kolder** is cited as the example of a "vibrant and punchy" style, but no first-party breakdown of his tools was found ([Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creators/tomas-pollen-stavik-6-lessons-on-how-to-approach-color-grading)).
- **The common creator stack** (documented for creators generally): Resolve with a film plugin; colourist Tomas Pollen Stavik uses Dehancer as "one layer in a wider process" ([Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creators/tomas-pollen-stavik-6-lessons-on-how-to-approach-color-grading)). Dehancer: 60+ film profiles, print films, grain, halation, bloom, film damage, overscan ([Dehancer shop](https://dehancer.com/shop), [Fstoppers review](https://fstoppers.com/reviews/dehancer-film-emulation-review-bringing-magic-film-your-digital-photography-673374)). FilmConvert Nitrate: pick camera profile, then film stock, then grain; profiles were built by shooting charts per camera, and grain is modelled per colour and exposure ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/in-depth-look-filmconvert-nitrate/), [FilmConvert](https://filmconvert.com/nitrate)). Resolve's own Film Look Creator (Studio only) bundles halation, bloom, grain, flicker, gate weave ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2024/08/15/what-is-resolves-new-film-look-creator-plugin/), [Skillshare listing](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/cinematic-look-made-easy-davinci-resolve-colorgrading-masterclass/57923291)).
- **Camera vs grade.** Practitioners say much of the look is in the capture: light, exposure, white balance, wardrobe (F1/F3/F4's red hoodie is a wardrobe choice the grade then exploits) ([Adorama](https://www.adorama.com/alc/how-to-make-your-youtube-videos-more-cinematic)). Correction must come first; creative LUTs "are not used to balance contrast or saturation" ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2019/08/12/luts-101/)).
- **Phone footage reality:**
  - *8-bit SDR (most phone and screen clips):* already tone-mapped and saturated in camera; FiLMiC's own notes say 8-bit log-ish capture is "susceptible to banding" once corrected ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/12/20/filmic-pro-ios-log-v2-dynamic-range/), [FiLMiC](https://www.filmicpro.com/products/cinekit/)). **Rec:** treat as "gentle push" footage: looks must work with modest curves; add dither/grain at output to hide banding.
  - *iPhone HDR (default HDR Video setting):* Dolby Vision Profile 8.4 with an **HLG base layer**, so any HEVC decoder can read it as HLG ([Apple developer PDF](https://developer.Apple.com/av-foundation/Incorporating-HDR-video-with-Dolby-Vision-into-your-apps.pdf), [Apple support](https://support.apple.com/HT211950)). Users report bad SDR conversions in mainstream editors ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/hdr-to-sdr-conversion/td-p/12470041), [Descript](https://help.descript.com/add-and-manage-media/ts-hdr)). This is our biggest quality opportunity: a good HLG→SDR path (R05 §A3, BT.2390) plus a grade gives more highlight room than 8-bit SDR.
  - *Apple Log (iPhone 15 Pro and later, ProRes):* BT.2020 primaries + an Apple-defined log curve ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corevideo/cvimagelogtransferfunction.md)); Apple Log 2 keeps the curve and changes the gamut to Apple Wide Gamut ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/log-cam-and-raw)). Not raw: "denoised, tone-mapped, and color-adjusted"; typically 10-bit; exposure/WB best done as offsets in log before the look ([Prolost](https://prolost.com/applelog)). CineD measured ~11–12 stops above the noise floor on iPhone 17 Pro ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/log-cam-and-raw)). Most latitude of any phone source.
  - *Samsung Log (S25 series):* 10-bit HEVC, curve described as "log function and an S-shaped curve"; Samsung publishes Log→Linear 1D and Log→Rec.709 3D LUTs ([Samsung Developer](https://developer.samsung.com/mobile/samsung-log-video.html)). No formula published that we found; the LUT terms of use must be checked before bundling.

## 3. How tools structure it (UX lessons; describe, never copy)

- **Resolve node order:** correction (balance, exposure) → secondaries → look → film emulation last; Dehancer says WB, wheels, curves and masks "should be located before Dehancer node" ([Dehancer node sequence](https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/dehancer-in-davinci-resolve-node-sequence/), [Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/davinci-resolve-color-grading)). Beginners trip over nodes (R03 table, [Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/davinci-resolve-color-grading-for-beginners/)). **Lesson:** keep the order, hide the nodes: three fixed layers **Fix → Look → Film**.
- **Resolve Color Warper:** a mesh over hue–saturation (and a chroma–luma panel) where you drag points to move colours, per-point hue/sat/luma ([Mixing Light](https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/color-warper-101-concepts-and-interface-fundamentals), [Mixing Light pt 2](https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/color-warper-101-part-2-managing-points-pins-chroma-luma-panel)). **Lesson:** a "drag a colour to where you want it" control is the advanced form of our hue bands.
- **Lightroom:** Preset Amount slider (June 2022, Classic 11.4), 0–200, only on presets that opt in ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/whats-new/2022-4.html), [PhotoFocus](https://photofocus.com/software/the-most-requested-lightroom-feature-is-here-a-look-at-the-preset-amount-slider)); Adaptive presets use AI masks (subject, sky, portrait) and recompute masks when pasted across photos ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/learn/lightroom-cc/web/optimize-workflow-with-adaptive-presets), [Adobe masking](https://helpx.adobe.com/id_en/lightroom/desktop/edit-photos/masking.html)). **Lesson:** strength slider on every look, up to 150–200 %; masks recompute per clip.
- **CapCut:** tap a filter, intensity slider in the same panel; applying to many clips is done via an adjustment layer ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/resource/capcut-filter), [CapCut adjustment layers](https://www.capcut.com/create/adjustment-layers-for-multiple-clips)). KineMaster has an explicit "Apply to all" ([KineMaster](https://kinemaster.com/features/color-grading)). **Lesson:** "Apply to all clips" as a first-class button, plus a project-level look.
- **Snapseed 3.0:** reorganised into Looks / Faves / Tools with film-inspired filters (R03, [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/06/13/snapseed-updated-with-film-inspired-filters-and-redesigned-edit-experience)).
- **VSCO / Dazz-style film apps:** VSCO presets come from "physical modeling" of film stocks and can be applied live while shooting ([VSCO help](https://intercom.help/vsco/en/articles/12698471), [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/au/tech/apps/the-retro-look-from-an-iphone-vsco-levels-up-the-film-look-with-a-free-app-that-applies-presets-before-you-shoot)); Dazz is liked for a simple interface and nostalgia, with paywalled cameras ([Shotkit](https://shotkit.com/film-camera-apps/), [MakeTechEasier](https://maketecheasier.com/iphone-apps-photos-look-like-film)). F1 is squarely this "retro digicam" aesthetic.
- **Fujifilm film simulations:** a short menu of named looks, each described in one line (e.g. Classic Chrome "soft color and enhanced shadow contrast for a calm look"; Classic Neg "enhanced color with hard tonality") ([Fujifilm](https://www.fujifilm-x.com/global/film-simulation/)). **Lesson:** one-line, mood-based descriptions; few, well-tuned looks beat hundreds.
- **Dehancer:** film profile first, print section only works with a film profile selected; halation and bloom "rarely appear separately" ([Dehancer tool profiles](https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/dehancer-tool-profiles)).
- **What beginners love:** one tap, instant result, presets that "just work" on phone footage, nostalgia ([Shotkit](https://shotkit.com/film-camera-apps/)). **What they hate / get wrong:** wrong input colour space giving harsh or over-saturated results, orange faces, 100 % strength "trashing" images (fix: ~50 %), LUT used instead of correction ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2019/08/12/luts-101/), [Videomaker](https://www.videomaker.com/news/you-should-avoid-these-5-color-grading-mistakes-at-all-costs/), [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/originals/why-using-blockbuster-luts-doesnt-make-your-footage-look-exactly-theatrical-381625)); grading intimidation (R03 #11). **Rec:** our looks are input-aware (no "wrong colour space" possible), skin-protected and tuned so 100 % is tasteful.

## 4. Technical design for our pipeline

### 4.1 Where colour sits relative to D5
```
decode → Y'CbCr→RGB (tagged) → INPUT TRANSFORM → scene-linear (AP1 or Rec.709 primaries, f16)
      → [per clip] FIX (exposure, WB, auto) → LOOK (in log grading space) → FILM (halation/bloom in linear; grain last)
      → DISPLAY RENDERING TRANSFORM (DRT) → display-linear Rec.709 → D5 compositor (titles, overlays, transitions) → encode BT.709
```
- **Rec:** grade per clip *before* the D5 compositor so titles and graphics are never graded (an opt-in "grade titles too" flag later). Film grain on the composited frame (project-level), so grain also sits over titles like real film; optional.
- **Input transforms:** Rec.709/sRGB (inverse BT.1886/sRGB, R05 §A3); HLG/PQ → scene-linear via the inverse OETF/EOTF (not tone-mapped to SDR first: the DRT does the mapping, so the grade sees the full HDR range); Apple Log via its published curve (white paper, Sept 2023; implemented in BSD-3 [colour-science](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/v0.4.5/_modules/colour/models/rgb/transfer_functions/apple_log_profile.html), licence [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/colour-science)) + BT.2020→working primaries; Samsung Log: no formula found, so either derive from Samsung's own 1D LUT (check its terms first) or a sampled fit we measure from our own test clips. Photos: sRGB/ICC (R05) through the identical path; RAW (M8) enters as scene-linear and simply skips the inverse step.
- **SDR sources and the DRT (key decision).** SDR footage is already display-referred. Running it through a filmic DRT would tone-map twice (dull, grey). **Rec:** make the DRT an *invertible* parametric sigmoid and feed SDR sources through its exact inverse (capped at ~+3 stops), so a neutral grade round-trips SDR to itself bit-for-bit within f16 tolerance (golden-frame test), while grades get scene-like highlight room. Log/HDR/RAW sources skip the inverse. One look then behaves the same on every source.
- **Working / grading space.** Blending, blur, halation and exposure in **scene-linear** (physically meaningful). Contrast, wheels, curves and the look's tone shaping in a **log grading space** (perceptually even, pivot at mid-grey), as ACEScct was designed for "smooth tonal adjustments" ([Wikipedia: ACES](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Academy_Color_Encoding_System)). ACES is now **Apache-2.0** under the Academy Software Foundation ([aces-aswf/aces](https://github.com/aces-aswf/aces), [Academy press](https://press.oscars.org/news/academy-motion-picture-arts-and-sciences-launches-next-chapter-aces-academy-software)), so **Rec:** adopt the ACEScct curve and AP1 primaries for grading (AP1 avoids clipping on saturation pushes), add the NOTICE. We do *not* need ACES output transforms: our own DRT (below) is simpler and tuned for SDR web delivery.
- **DRT / view transform.** AgX and Filmic (Troy Sobotka) are the reference designs for "graceful path to white" highlight handling, but the original repos ([sobotka/AgX](https://github.com/sobotka/AgX), [filmic-blender](https://github.com/sobotka/filmic-blender), [SB2383 generator](https://github.com/sobotka/SB2383-Configuration-Generation)) and Eary Chow's fork ([EaryChow/AgX](https://github.com/EaryChow/AgX)) show **no licence file**; Blender's AgX config points to an `ocio-license.txt` we have not verified ([Blender PR #106355](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/106355)). darktable implements sigmoid/filmic/AgX but is GPL. **Rec:** clean-room our own DRT from published concepts: log-logistic sigmoid on a norm (ratio-preserving, like darktable's "rgb ratio" description) + hue-preserving highlight desaturation toward white + small gamut compression. Copy no code or LUT data from AgX/darktable. OCIO is BSD-3-Clause ([OCIO docs](https://opencolorio.readthedocs.io/en/main/aswf/license.html)) if we ever want config interchange, but a full OCIO dependency is not needed for M-colour.

### 4.2 Primary ops (all GPU, WGSL, one fused pass where possible)
| Op | Space | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure (EV) | linear | multiply by 2^EV |
| White balance temp/tint | linear, LMS (CAT02/Bradford) | von Kries scale; temp in mireds so steps feel even |
| Lift/gamma/gain wheels + offset (log wheels) | log | offset = Prolost's "offsets in log before the LUT" ([Prolost](https://prolost.com/applelog)) |
| Contrast + pivot, toe/shoulder | log | pivot default 18 % grey (ACEScct ≈ 0.4135) |
| Curves (Y + RGB) | log | 1D LUT textures, 256–1024 entries |
| HSL: hue-vs-hue, hue-vs-sat, hue-vs-lum, lum-vs-sat | OKLCh ([Oklab](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)) | 8 band controls in basic UI; periodic spline in advanced |
| Vibrance / saturation | OKLCh | vibrance = gain weighted by (1 − C/Cmax) and skin mask |
| Split tone (shadow/highlight hue+amount, balance) | log, Oklab a/b offsets weighted by luminance | |
| Colour warper (later) | OKLCh mesh | the advanced "drag a colour" tool |
| Look LUT (.cube) | per-LUT declared input (default Rec.709 display or ACEScct) | tetrahedral |

- **.cube import:** Adobe's 2013 Cube LUT spec; `LUT_3D_SIZE` required, `DOMAIN_MIN/MAX` default 0/1, red index varies fastest ([colour-science reader](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/develop/_modules/colour/io/luts/iridas_cube.html), [format reference](https://zhjngli.github.io/AgX/reference/concepts/lut-format.html)). Validate strictly (size 2–256, exact entry count, finite floats, file ≤ 64 MB) — it's untrusted input. **Interpolation:** tetrahedral preserves the grey axis better than trilinear and needs 4 taps vs 8 ([libplacebo MR](https://code.videolan.org/videolan/libplacebo/-/merge_requests/287), [lcms list](https://mail-archive.com/lcms-user@lists.sourceforge.net/msg03610.html)); hardware does trilinear and After Effects silently falls back to it ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-beta-discussions/discuss-high-precision-tetrahedral-lut-processing/m-p/14625822)). **Rec:** manual tetrahedral in WGSL from a 3D texture with `textureLoad` (no filtering), always; ask the user which space the LUT expects (Rec.709 display / Apple Log / ACEScct), default Rec.709 display, and apply it *after* our DRT-inverse-aware conversion so a creator's downloaded "Rec.709" LUT behaves as they expect.
- **Look strength.** Two mechanisms: (a) parametric looks scale every parameter toward identity (contrast 1 + s·(c−1), offsets × s, etc.) — exact, smooth, and the same on photos; (b) LUT looks blend the result with the input **in the log grading space** (perceptually even; linear-light mixing over-weights highlights). Range 0–150 % (Lightroom goes to 200 %, [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/whats-new/2022-4.html)); for >100 %, (a) extrapolates parameters, (b) extrapolates the difference with clamping.
- **Skin protect (on by default).** Soft hue qualifier in OKLCh centred on the skin hue (default ≈ OKLCh hue 50–60°, adapted per clip by finding the dominant cluster near the 123° vectorscope line), width ±25°, chroma 0.02–0.15, luminance-limited, feathered; the mask attenuates look hue rotations and saturation pushes (default 70 % protection), never exposure/contrast. Later: face/person segmentation (M6 background-removal model) to restrict the mask spatially. Show the mask with a "show protected skin" toggle.
- **Auto fix.** Auto exposure: set the log-average luminance to mid-grey with highlight guard (99.5th percentile under the shoulder). Auto WB: grey-world/white-patch hybrid in linear, with skin cluster as a sanity check (reject corrections that pull skin off the line). Both run on a 256×144 downsample.
- **Match to reference / shot match.** Statistics transfer in a decorrelated perceptual space (the classic Reinhard et al. colour transfer, [IEEE CG&A 2001](https://doi.org/10.1109/38.946629)) done in Oklab: match mean and std of L, a, b, plus percentile-matched tone curve (black/median/white), then *convert the result into our own op parameters* (exposure, WB, contrast, split tone, saturation) so the user can see and tweak what "match" did. Reference can be another clip, a photo, or a frame the user imports (kept local, never stored in the project unless they add it as media). Shot match across a scene = match each clip's Fix layer to a hero clip, leave the Look shared (Resolve's "shared starting point" idea, [Larry Jordan](https://larryjordan.com/articles/how-to-color-match-clips-in-davinci-resolve-20/)).

### 4.3 Film layer (physically motivated, cheap)
- **Halation:** threshold scene-linear luminance (soft knee, default 2.0× diffuse white for log/HDR, 0.9 after inverse-DRT for SDR), edge-weight it (high-pass of the thresholded mask, since halation is an edge effect), blur with a downsampled pyramid, tint red→orange by intensity, add back in linear before the DRT. Controls: amount, radius, threshold ([Dehancer](https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/halation/), [Boris FX colour ramp](https://borisfx.com/documentation/sapphire/ofx/halation/)). Measured target (F1): warm fringe of ~15–20 code values R−B adjacent to clipped areas.
- **Bloom:** neutral, wider, lower amount, same pyramid (share the passes).
- **Grain:** procedural, luminance-dependent amplitude (peak in mids, less in deep shadows and highlights), per-channel slightly decorrelated, size in output pixels, temporally new per frame, seeded by frame index for deterministic exports. A fast Boolean-model approximation ([Newson et al.](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2017/192/)) or a precomputed tileable noise texture with per-pixel modulation; also acts as dither against banding on 8-bit sources.
- **Vignette** (elliptical, in linear, exposure-style), **gate weave** (sub-pixel low-frequency jitter, off by default), **matte** (2.39 / 2.0 / 1.85 bars or crop).

### 4.4 GPU cost on Iris Xe
Iris Xe 96 EU ≈ 2.15 TFLOPS FP32, memory shared with system RAM and needing dual-channel to run at full speed ([TechPowerUp](https://techpowerup.com/gpu-specs/iris-xe-graphics-96eu-mobile.c3881), [Intel Xe-LP arch](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/oneapi/optimization-guide-gpu/2023-0/intel-iris-xe-gpu-architecture.html), [cpu-monkey](https://www.cpu-monkey.com/en/igpu-intel_iris_xe_graphics_96_tiger_lake)). **Estimate (ours, to be benchmarked):** a fused grade pass (~300–500 FLOPs/px incl. tetrahedral LUT) is ~1 GFLOP at 1080p — compute is not the limit; bandwidth is. One `Rgba16Float` read+write at 1080p is ~33 MB, so with a few tens of GB/s shared bandwidth each full-res pass costs on the order of 1 ms. Budget at 1080p preview: input + fused Fix/Look pass + DRT folded in (1–2 passes), halation/bloom pyramid at ½…1/32 res (~0.4 full-pass equivalents), grain fused into the final pass: **≈ 3–5 ms/frame**, comfortably inside 33 ms. 4K export is ~4× (offline, fine); 4K *preview* should use the existing proxy path (1080p or ½-res). **Rec:** fuse everything except the blur pyramid into one uber-shader generated per look (only enabled ops), keep LUTs as 33³ 3D textures (≈ 290 KB in f16).

### 6.3 UX
- **Looks strip** with live thumbnails rendered from the *current* frame (tiny 192×108 GPU renders), one tap applies to the selected clip(s); **strength** slider (0–150 %, default 100 %) appears in place.
- **Skin protect** chip, on by default, with "show protected area".
- **Before/after:** hold `\` (R03 convention) for full before; draggable split-view line; side-by-side for photos.
- **Scope:** "This clip" (default) / "Selected" / "Whole project" (project look applied under per-clip overrides); "Apply to all" button (KineMaster pattern).
- **Copy/paste grade** (Ctrl+Alt+C / Ctrl+Alt+V), paste options: Fix, Look, Film, or all; pasting re-runs auto masks per clip (Lightroom adaptive pattern).
- **Match:** "Match to…" another clip, a photo, or an imported reference frame; result lands as editable Fix parameters.
- **Depth on demand:** Basic (Light, Colour, Look strength, Film) → Advanced (wheels, curves, hue bands, split tone, LUT) → later Pro (colour warper, scopes). Same panel, grows (R03).
- **Save look:** user looks are the same JSON; export/import `.editelier-look`; `.cube` import as a look's LUT slot.
- **Photos:** identical looks and panel (M3's "looks first" scope gets this for free).

### 6.4 Where colour should move in the milestone order
Current PLAN: M1 cut/export → M2 multitrack → M3 photos → **M4 colour**. Argument for moving it up:
1. **It is the product.** The owner's end goal is graded edits; R03 already found grading intimidation is a top pain and "looks before knobs" the fix. A free editor with great one-tap looks is differentiated; one with multitrack is not.
2. **Architecture cost.** Where the grade sits relative to D5's compositor, the SDR inverse-DRT round-trip, HLG handling and per-clip vs project state are pipeline decisions. Retrofitting them after M2/M3 means re-validating every golden frame twice.
3. **Photos inherit it.** M3 scope already says "looks first"; building Looks before M3 makes photos nearly free.

**Rec (proposal for the owner, not applied to PLAN.md):**
- **M1 (unchanged scope, +1 small item):** an identity Grade node and the invertible DRT in the render graph, with the neutral round-trip golden test. Cheap now, expensive later.
- **New M2 "Looks":** Fix (exposure, WB, auto), the 12 looks, strength, skin protect, film layer (halation/bloom/grain/vignette/matte), before/after, copy/paste, apply-to-all/project look, HLG input done properly, Apple Log input. Exit: look golden frames + skin-line check + Iris Xe 1080p preview ≤ 8 ms grade cost.
- **M3 multitrack, M4 photos** (shifted by one).
- **M5 "Pro colour":** wheels/curves/HSL advanced panel, `.cube` import with tetrahedral, scopes (`filmcraft-scopes`), match-to-reference, colour warper later; RAW stays M8 (enters the same pipeline as scene-linear).

## Summary (12 lines)
1. Measured: all four reference frames share a lifted, teal-leaning black floor (10–16/255, red lowest), low overall saturation (C* 7–17) with 1–2 hero colours at 2–3× median chroma, and warm-subject/cool-environment separation.
2. F1 = overexposed retro film: 14 % clipped, blue shadows/yellow-green highlights, hot red, warm halation fringe; F2 = muted cool fade with soft shoulder and warm autumn accents; F3 = 81 % shadows, matte, cyan-teal held highlights; F4 = low-key warm, no whites (max 150), skin ~20° yellow of the I-line.
3. Frames 2–4 are 16:9 inside the phone's pillarbox; no letterbox in the grade itself. Grain is unmeasurable through YouTube compression.
4. "Vibrant cinematic" = S-curve with highlight roll-off, faded blacks, complementary split, selective (not global) saturation, skin on the line, plus halation/bloom/grain.
5. No public workflow for eyes of leo (@shotbywang) or the other named creators was found; the creator stack generally is Resolve + Dehancer/FilmConvert-style film layers after correction.
6. Phone reality: 8-bit SDR takes gentle pushes only; iPhone HDR is HLG-based (big opportunity with a proper HLG→SDR path); Apple Log (10-bit ProRes, published curve) has most latitude; Samsung Log has LUTs but no published formula.
7. UX lesson from Resolve/Lightroom/CapCut/Fujifilm/Dehancer: fixed hidden order Fix → Look → Film, few well-named looks, strength slider (to 150 %), apply-to-all, paste with per-clip re-masking.
8. Pipeline: grade per clip in scene-linear (AP1) with log (ACEScct, now Apache-2.0) for tone ops, before D5's compositor; own clean-room invertible sigmoid DRT so SDR round-trips unchanged.
9. Ops in OKLCh for hue/sat/skin; .cube import with strict validation and manual tetrahedral WGSL; strength by parameter scaling (or log-space mix for LUTs); skin protect on by default.
10. Iris Xe estimate: ~3–5 ms/frame at 1080p for a fused grade + pyramid halation/bloom + grain; 4K preview via proxies.
11. Legal: ship only our own parameter looks (MIT); AgX/Filmic repos have no licence file and darktable is GPL, so concepts only; no third-party LUTs/HaldCLUTs or film-stock names.
12. Proposal: add an identity grade node + DRT test to M1 and make "Looks" the new M2 (12 looks, film layer, skin protect, before/after, copy/paste, HLG/Apple Log), push multitrack/photos one slot, and keep wheels/curves/LUT/scopes/match as "Pro colour" later.
